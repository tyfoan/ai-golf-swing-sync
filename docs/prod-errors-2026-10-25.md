Generated by the `prod-errors` skill, two days after 1.3.5 (2) was submitted. Questions: did 1.3.5

## Verdict

**1.3.5 is live (READY_FOR_SALE) and has no crash or hang signal so far, but the numbers are tiny.**
Apple approved it sometime between 23/10 19:01 UTC (the reviewer's last event) and 24/10
07:40 UTC (the first launch by a real user). Four devices have run 1.3.5, all fresh installs: two App
Review devices, one test install that ran Restore Purchases, and one organic user. **No existing
1.3.4 user has updated yet**, so the crash-loop fix is unproven in the field.

The only error signal on 1.3.5 is `camera_config_failed`: 13 events, all on two iPhone 15 Pro
devices, the App Review device and the test install. The camera code did not change between 1.3.4
and 1.3.5, so this is not a regression. The organic user on an iPhone 17 Pro Max recorded, saved and
compared swings with no errors.

The Lifetime buyer opened 1.3.4 on 24/10 and **was not shown a paywall**: no crash, no upgrade prompt.
The camera failed to start twice in that one-second session.

## Findings

1. **`camera_config_failed` on 1.3.5, iPhone 15 Pro only (P2, watch).**
   - App Review device, iOS 27.0.1, 23/10 18:58–19:01 UTC: 8 × `start_running`, meaning the
     session configured but never started. The reviewer kept retrying, then went to Settings and
     the paywall. Apple approved anyway.
   - Test install, iOS 26.6.2, 24/10 21:59–22:00 UTC: 5 × `noVideoDevice`, meaning
     `AVCaptureDevice.default(.builtInWideAngleCamera, …)` returned nil, right after onboarding and
     again after Restore Purchases. On a real iPhone this usually means the camera is restricted
     (Screen Time or MDM), in use by the system, or unavailable during iPhone Mirroring.
   - Past releases show the same pattern: 1.3.3's review window (02/08) logged 4 × `noVideoDevice`.
     App Review devices regularly have no working camera, and a rejection under guideline 2.1 is
     possible if a reviewer ever insists on recording.
   - Not a regression: `Services/CameraService.swift` and `Services/Camera/` are identical in
     1.3.4 (`43d5d4f`) and 1.3.5 (`1fd6592`).
2. **The Lifetime buyer: no crash, no paywall on 24/10 (good sign, not proof).** On 1.3.4: app
   launched, main screen, 2 × `camera_config_failed start_running` within one second, then nothing.
   No `paywall_shown`, which suggests the entitlement is now seen. They are still on 1.3.4.
3. **An open-ended recording on the organic 1.3.5 user (weak).** A `recording_started` at 24/10
   09:12 UTC has no stop or save after it. The next launch came 1 h 45 min later. This could be a
   user who walked away, or the old finalize-freeze fingerprint. `recording_finalize_timeout` and
   `hang_detected` have never been ingested, so the data can't tell these apart.
4. **Export is invisible in analytics.** `export_started`, `export_completed` and `export_failed`
   had 0 events in 90 days. Either nobody exports or the export path is not instrumented, and the
   cream export redesign can't be measured until this is resolved.

## Gaps

- Real users have not updated to 1.3.5 yet, so the crash-loop fix is unproven. Check again around
  01/11, when automatic updates have rolled out.
- `camera_config_failed` sends only the error case. Sending `AVCaptureDevice.authorizationStatus`
  and the session's interruption reason with it would tell restricted, busy and broken cameras apart.
- No real-time crash reporting (no Sentry project).
- The `perfPowerMetrics` call in `asc_diagnostics.py` needs the right Accept header before power
  and hang-rate metrics can be read.
